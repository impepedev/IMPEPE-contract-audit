# IMPEPE threat model and review boundaries

## Assets and authorities

Creation IMD, protocol fees, NFT artwork and holder rewards have separate destinations. The admin seals token exclusions once. Phase I creation-fund recovery and controller replacement require public 48-hour schedules; see EMERGENCY_MIGRATION.md. Replacement-code approval adds an explicit administrator trust boundary. The Operator pays bounded job budgets and submits evidence. The independent attestor authorizes the exact encoded artwork, timing/effect metadata, token ID, request, controller, chain and expiry. Its address must differ from the Operator and admin. Initial single-sided liquidity is permanently locked: neither its owner nor the admin can remove it. Constructor, opening price and bootstrap configuration still require a careful review before sealing.

## Verified locally

Compile and EIP-170 bytecode limits; 98/2 fixed genesis supply; sealed exclusions; historical cutoff despite post-cutoff sale; exact static NFT #1; proof rejection; direct collection access rejection; actual v4 PoolManager single-sided seeding in both currency orientations; zero initial real IMD; permanent lock after a ten-year time advance; outside additions/removals rejected; buy/sell fee accounting; atomic and permissionless claim settlement; swap deadline/slippage rejection; exact-output rejection; seeded #999 final mint and no #1001; automatic phase routing and surplus settlement; proportional claims; seller accrual and buyer future accrual; scoped artifact signature/expiry; raw-frame constraints; Ed25519 signature mutation/lease rejection; SQL score accounting and rollback; canonical job reservation/admission; quote-bound payment policy; encrypted outbox integrity; missing-feed behavior.

These are implementation tests, not an independent security audit or 1000 individual mint simulations. The final transition test seeds #999 state; it does not claim the first 999 works were generated. Device-signature tests use local keys and protocol preimages, not a real IMD completed job receipt.

## Outstanding launch risks

1. Native IMD evidence: artifact uploads are device-signed, but the public download route alone does not prove accepted-job provenance or expose a complete authenticated receipt. Establish how accepted submissions, leases, device keys, RGB data and animation metadata bind together. The independent EIP-712 signer bridge is approved. Its public address is still needed. The accepted-result/submission adapter and separate signer service are implemented and locally tested; they trust official IMD HTTPS evidence and RPC finality, not native on-chain IMD proof.
2. Finality: selection requires both a block delay and a signed cutoff-block finality attestation scoped to this controller, chain and expiry. Recent hashes are checked against blockhash. The worker also checks the finalized RPC block. This trusted attestation is not a beacon consensus proof; the user approved the independent signer model. An unavailable signer halts selection, and a compromised signer can falsely attest finality.
3. Ranking cost: selection scans all historically qualifying holders per original allocation. Registry admission and cutoff eligibility require 10,000 IMPEPE under the owner's audit-remediation decision. Dust transfers cannot enter the registry, but qualifying balances can be recycled across addresses and entries persist. Batch sizes cap transaction gas, not total work. Keeper cost and availability remain accepted design risks; this threshold is not a Sybil proof
4. Failed work/recovery: selection reservations are permanent, and jobs are serialized. A failed paid IMD order can be recovered only with an independently scoped recovery approval and the exact prior budget refunded to the controller. The same NFT number and recipient remain reserved; old request IDs cannot be reused. Direct minting skips receiver callbacks as approved, so a rejecting contract cannot halt delivery but may be unable to recover its NFT. Fully scanned empty snapshots advance only to the earliest later funding block and require renewed finality. A vanished Operator, unavailable attestor or unavailable refund can still halt creation. Do not silently substitute another wallet or draw replacement art in the Operator.
5. Payment integration: SDK policy is tested with controlled challenges. A real paid job and recovery test have not run. Match the live input hash, quote expiry, Permit2 spender and payment recipient before signing. Pricing is deployment-fixed; changed admission prices intentionally stop payments.
6. Liquidity and quotes: single-sided liquidity starts with zero real IMD. A permanent lock does not guarantee market value or exits; the deployer's separate 2% can be sold after buyers fund the pool. Only exact-input swaps that fully consume their input are supported. The tested IMPEPESwapRouter flushes fee claims atomically after settlement and checks minimum output/deadline. External v4 routers can leave claims pending until permissionless flush; the worker flushes before opening jobs, and funding cutoffs use actual deposits. Keeper liveness, canonical Quoter/Universal Router compatibility and frontend signing still need live end-to-end verification. The configured pool LP fee is zero beyond the hook's 4%; governance can enable a separate Uniswap protocol fee. Trading remains available and quotes incorporate that additional external cost. The target opening valuation is approved at 1,000 IMD; verify its derived tick against final addresses and on-chain initialization. There is no position recovery or migration path under this permanent lock.
7. Backend operations: database backups, production PostgreSQL deployment, HTTPS, process supervision, rate limits, RPC failover and observability need deployment validation. Finalized-history mismatch halts for manual rebuild; it never silently resets scores. Production workers share a PostgreSQL advisory lock and durable signed-mint outbox. Lock behavior still requires validation on production PostgreSQL; keep the Operator dedicated and coordinate manual recovery.
8. Art acceptance: 8 frames and 2000-20000ms are provisional measured bounds. Exact trait occurrence limits, monotonic creative development and a final-only Holy Grail signature need creative acceptance rules. The contracts enforce geometry and uniqueness, not artistic quality. Test SMIL playback on intended NFT viewers.
9. Key compromise: a compromised Operator can spend the currently released budget and stall work; it cannot forge the independent artifact signature. Compromise of the bridge attestor can authorize non-agent artwork. Administrator key custody and bridge signer custody must be decided without turning the established wallet into an unattended hot key.
10. Review status: the IMD audit of commit cafc305e764f0300c8ddf9d70feea2113a58b913 is complete. The owner accepts locally verified v1.2 remediation without another audit. See AUDIT_REMEDIATION.md and artifacts/audit-approval.json. Specialist fee-sharing, regulatory and IP review remains a separate deployment input

## Emergency migration boundary

The admin can pause creation immediately, recover remaining controller IMD to the immutable original deployer after 48 hours, and nominate a replacement controller after a separate 48-hour delay. A compromised admin can misuse this delayed power; events/delay do not prove failure or force redeposit. The supplied replacement atomically redeposits the full recovered amount, preserves historical allocations and current paid work, and forwards fee escrow. Already-paid external budgets remain separately exposed. Pool liquidity, existing NFTs and accrued rewards remain outside withdrawal. Controller ancestry is capped at eight migrations. A bug in the immutable token, pool/hook, collection or rewards implementation is not solved by this path.
